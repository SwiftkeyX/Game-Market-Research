# Subgenre Guide — Roguelike / Roguelite

Data current as of early 2026. Always verify with a web search before using in a client deliverable.

---

## Roguelike Deckbuilder

**Steam tag**: `roguelike-deckbuilder`
**Games on Steam**: ~1,385
**2025 hit rate**: 5.1% (11 of 212 releases hit 1000+ reviews)
**Trend**: 📈 Growing — fastest-growing roguelite sub in 2023–2024

**Why it works for small teams**:
- Art bar is low if mechanics are exceptional (Slice & Dice uses pure abstraction)
- Systems-heavy design plays to programmer strengths
- High replayability baked in by nature of deckbuilding
- Audience is omnivorous — will buy many deckbuilders

**Solo benchmarks** (achievable):
- Slice & Dice — solo dev, abstract pixel art, $4.99, $1M+ revenue
- Balatro — solo dev, minimalist card visuals, $14.99, $30M+ revenue (outlier, but shows ceiling)

**Team benchmarks** (3–10 people):
- Monster Train — ~10 people, stylized 2D, $24.99, $5M+
- Monster Train 2 — ~10 people, $3.8M in launch month (May 2025)

**Key risks**:
- Perceived as crowded (though data shows it's not as crowded as feared)
- Must have a strong mechanical hook — "just another deckbuilder" won't cut it
- Hook must be explainable in one sentence

## Tactical / Strategic Roguelite

**Steam tag**: `turn-based-tactics`, `strategy`, `turn-based-strategy`
**2025 hit rate**: ~3–4% (estimated)
**Trend**: 📈 Growing

**Why it works**:
- Depth can substitute for visual polish
- Abstract/minimal art is accepted by the audience
- Smaller, more dedicated player base = easier to WOM

**Solo benchmarks**:
- Slice & Dice — abstract visuals, dice tactics, $1M+
- Into the Breach — 2-person team (borderline solo-scale), $10M+

**Team benchmarks**:
- 9 Kings — small team, roguelike kingdom-builder hybrid, $2.2M in May 2025 launch month alone

## Action Roguelite

**Steam tag**: `action-roguelike`
**2025 hit rate**: ~2–3% (estimated)
**Trend**: ➡️ Stable

**Why it's hard solo**:
- Hades / Dead Cells set a visual and feel standard that takes teams to match
- Controls/feedback must be tight — players notice bad game feel immediately
- Scope creep is common and deadly

**Solo viable only if**:
- Extremely narrow scope (1 biome, few enemies, short run)
- Distinctive visual style that doesn't require hand-illustrated art
- Exceptional controls/feel as primary differentiator

**Team benchmarks** (3–6 people):
- Dead Cells — Motion Twin (~10 people), $30M+
- Risk of Rain 2 — Hopoo Games (~4 people originally), $30M+
- Hades II — Supergiant Games (~20 people) — NOT a direct benchmark for small teams

## Bullet Heaven / Survivors

**Steam tag**: `bullet-hell`, `action-roguelike` (often combined)
**2025 hit rate**: <1%
**Trend**: 📉 DECLINING — market collapsed after 2023

**Data**:
- 2022: 10 games hit 1000+ reviews
- 2023: 17 games hit 1000+ reviews (peak)
- 2024: 1 game hit 1000+ reviews (Deep Rock Galactic Survivor)
- 2025: market still saturated

**Verdict**: Only attempt if the concept is radically different from Vampire Survivors. "Survivors but with X theme" is not enough differentiation.

**Solo benchmarks** (for historical reference):
- Brotato — solo dev, simple pixel art, $4.99, $5M+ (but released in 2023 when market was open)
- Vampire Survivors — ~solo dev originally, $4.99, $80M+ (outlier — lightning-in-a-bottle)

## Hybrid / Genre Mashup

**Steam tag**: varies
**2025 hit rate**: Unknown — too few comps
**Trend**: 🔥 Opportunity — differentiation built-in

**Examples**:
- Balatro — poker + deckbuilder ($30M+, solo dev)
- Blue Prince — puzzle + exploration
- 9 Kings — kingdom builder + roguelike deckbuilder ($2.2M+ in May 2025)
- Crypt of the NecroDancer — rhythm + dungeon crawler

**Key requirements**:
- The hybrid must create something genuinely new, not just "two genres glued together"
- One-sentence hook must be instantly clear
- High risk because no direct comparables = hard to validate

**When to attempt**: When the team has a specific, testable mechanical insight, not as a default "let's be different" strategy.

## General Rules Across Subgenres

1. **Filter competitors to last 3 years** — older games reflect a different market
2. **Solo dev**: Stick to deckbuilder, tactical, or hybrid with minimal art requirements
3. **Team of 4**: Action roguelite or hybrid mashup becomes viable
4. **Never benchmark against outliers** — Hades, Balatro, Vampire Survivors are cultural moments, not templates
5. **The real success story** is polished, well-scoped games in high-demand genres, making $200K–$2M, that most casual gamers have never heard of
