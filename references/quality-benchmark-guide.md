# Quality Benchmark Scoring Guide

How to score each of the 8 dimensions when comparing competitors in a genre.
Score 1–10. Be brutally honest — the market will be.

---

## Two Types of Dimensions

| Type | Dimensions | How to score |
|---|---|---|
| **Observable** | Art, Store Page, Trailer, UI, Mechanical Hook | Score directly from the store page, screenshots, and trailer |
| **Review-required** | Game Feel, Content Depth, Replayability | Read player reviews (Steam, Reddit); each score **must include a citation** |

Observable scores are estimates — the `/gameplay-review` Excel output includes clickable store/screenshot/trailer URLs so you can verify them manually.

---

## Observable Dimensions

### Dimension 1: Art Style & Visual Quality

**Source**: Store page screenshots
**What to assess**: First impression when a player sees a screenshot. Does it look like a game worth paying for?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Programmer art. Placeholder-looking. Would embarrass the game on the store page. |
| 4–5 | Functional but generic. Players won't be impressed but won't be turned off. |
| 6–7 | Consistent, intentional style. Looks like a finished product. |
| 8–9 | Eye-catching. Strong visual identity. Players would wishlist based on screenshots alone. |
| 10 | Hades-level hand-illustrated beauty. Sets industry benchmarks. |

**Note**: Abstract/minimal art (Slice & Dice, Into the Breach) can score 7–8 if the style is *intentional and consistent*. "Minimal" is not the same as "unfinished."

---

### Dimension 2: Store Page / Screenshots

**Source**: Steam store page
**What to assess**: Does the store page convert browsers to wishlists? Players decide in ~10 seconds.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Few screenshots, bad composition, or screenshots that don't show the fun. |
| 4–5 | Adequate screenshots but they don't clearly communicate the game loop. |
| 6–7 | Good screenshots showing actual gameplay, clear genre signal. |
| 8–9 | Screenshots that make players immediately understand AND want the game. |
| 10 | Store page is a piece of marketing in itself. Every pixel is intentional. |

**Key test**: Show the store page to someone who hasn't heard of the game. Can they describe the genre and appeal in 10 seconds?

---

### Dimension 3: Trailer / Video Quality

**Source**: Launch trailer (YouTube or Steam)
**What to assess**: The launch trailer is your biggest marketing asset. Does it show the fun immediately?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | No trailer, or trailer is a slideshow of screenshots. |
| 4–5 | Gameplay is shown but pacing is slow or it takes >30s to get to the action. |
| 6–7 | Shows the core loop clearly. Decent music. Gets to the fun within 15 seconds. |
| 8–9 | Hooky opening, clear fun signal, strong music, leaves the player wanting to play immediately. |
| 10 | Viral-level trailer that communicates the game's unique selling point in the first 5 seconds. |

---

### Dimension 4: UI Clarity & Polish

**Source**: In-game screenshots / gameplay video
**What to assess**: Does the in-game UI look finished? Bad UI signals an unfinished game to players.

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | Default engine UI. Placeholder fonts. Misaligned elements. |
| 4–5 | Functional but clearly not designed. Gets the job done. |
| 6–7 | Clean, readable, consistent. Looks intentional. |
| 8–9 | UI enhances the aesthetic. Animations, feedback states, clear hierarchy. |
| 10 | UI is a design achievement — Balatro-level tactile card feel. |

---

### Dimension 5: Mechanical Hook (Uniqueness)

**Source**: Store page description
**What to assess**: Can the game's core concept be explained in one sentence? Is it genuinely new?

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | "It's a roguelite deckbuilder." No differentiator. |
| 4–5 | Has a twist but it's not compelling or easily explained. |
| 6–7 | Clear hook that distinguishes it from competitors. One sentence possible. |
| 8–9 | Hook is so clear and compelling it practically markets itself. Players immediately get why it's different. |
| 10 | "Poker but you rig the deck" — Balatro. Immediate, unique, intriguing. |

**The one-sentence test**: Write the hook in one sentence. If you can't, the concept needs more work.

---

## Review-Required Dimensions

These 3 dimensions **cannot be reliably scored from a store page**. They require reading actual
player reviews. Each score must include a citation: the platform (Steam, Reddit) and a short
quote that justifies the number.

If reviews are unavailable: write `N/A` and note why. A missing score is better than a fabricated one.

---

### Dimension 6: Game Feel (Controls, Feedback)

**Source**: Steam reviews, Reddit threads, YouTube review videos
**What to look for**: Player language around controls, responsiveness, "juicy" feedback, satisfying actions.
For card/strategy games: animation quality, sound effects on actions, satisfaction of winning a run.

| Score | Description | Review signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Floaty, unresponsive, no feedback. Players feel disconnected. | "clunky", "unresponsive", "doesn't feel good" |
| 4–5 | Functional. Doesn't get in the way but doesn't impress. | "fine", "works but nothing special" |
| 6–7 | Feels good to play. Controls are learnable and responsive. | "smooth", "good controls", "satisfying" |
| 8–9 | Satisfying feedback loops. Players feel competent and rewarded. | "love how it feels", "so satisfying", "tight controls" |
| 10 | Dead Cells / Balatro level — every input is crisp, every result feels earned. | "best game feel I've experienced", repeated in top reviews |

**Citation format**: `"[short quote]" — Steam review` or `"[quote]" — Reddit r/[subreddit]`

---

### Dimension 7: Content Depth / Scope

**Source**: Steam reviews, hour count reports, Reddit discussions
**What to look for**: Hours played, value-for-money comments, "felt short", "tons of content".

| Score | Description | Review signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 1–2 hours. Players feel cheated. | "too short", "done in 2 hours", "not worth the price" |
| 4–5 | 3–5 hours. Acceptable for very cheap games ($2.99 or below). | "short but fair for the price" |
| 6–7 | 8–15 hours. Adequate for $4.99–$9.99. | "good value", "10+ hours for the price" |
| 8–9 | 20–50+ hours. Strong value at $9.99–$19.99. | "40 hours in, still going", "so much content" |
| 10 | Slay the Spire level — hundreds of hours, still finding new synergies after year 2. | "300 hours, no end in sight" |

**Scope vs. team size**: A tight 10-hour deckbuilder is better than an unfinished 50-hour one.

---

### Dimension 8: Replayability

**Source**: Steam reviews, Reddit discussions, "hours played" reports
**What to look for**: Players returning after the first win, "one more run" language, run diversity.

| Score | Description | Review signals |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Every run feels the same. No reason to play again after first win. | "no replayability", "same every time", "beat it once, done" |
| 4–5 | Some variation but patterns repeat quickly. | "a few runs then got repetitive" |
| 6–7 | Multiple viable build paths. Each run has different key decisions. | "decent variety", "keeps me coming back" |
| 8–9 | Build diversity is deep. Players discover new strategies after 30+ hours. | "still finding new builds at 50 hours" |
| 10 | Virtually infinite replayability. Players report "one more run" at 2am. | "200 hours, still not bored", "one more run syndrome" |

---

## Scoring Interpretation

After scoring all 8 dimensions:

- **Any dimension ≤ 4**: Critical gap. The market will punish you for it.
- **Your average < 6**: Not ready to enter the revenue bracket.
- **Your average ≥ 7, no score < 5**: Competitive. Execute and launch.
- **Competitor scored 2+ points above you on any dimension**: Close that gap or accept lower revenue.

**Key principle**: Every aspect of your game should be competitive with the best-looking titles
in your revenue bracket. One weak link can kill the whole game.

## Total Score Flags

| Flag | Criteria |
|---|---|
| `STRONG` | HIGH tier game |
| `DECENT` | MID tier, total ≥ 50 |
| `WEAK` | MID tier, total < 50 |
| `LOW` | FAILURE tier |
